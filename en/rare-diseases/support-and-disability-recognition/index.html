Rare diseases

Support and disability recognition

What rights and support exist in Spain for people with a rare or genetic disease and their families, and how to apply.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 20 November 2026

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

At a glance

Procedure What it is for Where to apply
Disability certificate Access to tax, employment, education and social benefits Regional social services
Dependency assessment Services and benefits for people who need help with daily living Regional social services
CUME benefit Reduced working hours to care for a child with a serious illness Social Security or mutual insurer
Educational support Adaptations and resources at school School and guidance teams

Details vary by region and change with legal reforms, so it is always worth checking with social services or a patient organisation. Other European countries have broadly similar systems.

Disability certificate

It recognises a degree of disability as a percentage. From 33 % most rights apply: income tax reductions, reserved places in public employment, housing or transport support, and a parking badge if there are mobility problems, among others.

  • It is requested from the region's assessment centres (centros base), with medical reports.
  • A team assesses not only the diagnosis but how the condition affects daily life.
  • Since 2023 a new assessment scale, approved by Royal Decree 888/2022, has been applied, which takes more account of environmental barriers and social participation.
  • A confirmed genetic diagnosis is not needed: limitations are what is assessed.

Dependency

Law 39/2006 on Dependency recognises the right to support for those who need another person's help with basic activities of daily living. Depending on the grade (I, II or III), people can access telecare, home help, day centres, residential places or a payment for care in the family setting.

Caring for a child with a serious illness

The benefit for caring for children affected by cancer or another serious illness (known as CUME) allows one parent to reduce their working hours by at least half and receive a benefit that compensates for lost pay, while the child needs direct, continuous and permanent care. In some cases it extends up to age 23. Many rare diseases are on the list.

At school

Children with rare diseases may need educational support: curriculum adaptations, physiotherapy or speech therapy at school, assistants or school nursing. These are arranged through the school's guidance team. It is also useful to prepare an emergency plan.

Medicines

  • Approved and funded orphan medicines are usually dispensed by the hospital pharmacy.
  • When a drug is not yet authorised in Spain, the use of medicines in special situations (compassionate use) route, regulated by Royal Decree 1015/2009, is requested by the doctor.
  • Special dietary products, such as those for metabolic diseases, are covered under certain conditions.

Support for families

As well as official support, patient organisations advise on procedures, offer psychological support and put families with the same condition in touch. FEDER's information and guidance service is free.
