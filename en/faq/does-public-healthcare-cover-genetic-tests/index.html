FAQ

Does public healthcare cover genetic tests?

Yes, when there is a medical reason. In Spain genetic tests are part of the National Health System's common portfolio and are requested through specialists.

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

Short answer

Yes, when there is a medical reason. In Spain, genetic tests have been part of the National Health System's common portfolio of services since 2014. They are requested through a specialist or a clinical genetics unit, not on your own initiative. Most European public health systems work in a similar way.

What is included

The Spanish public system covers, among others:

  • Diagnosis of people with symptoms of a possible genetic condition, including panels, exome and, increasingly, genome sequencing.
  • Testing of relatives of a person with a known variant (cascade testing).
  • Prenatal diagnosis and newborn screening.
  • Preimplantation testing for serious conditions, subject to requirements.
  • Genetic testing of tumours to choose treatment.
  • Genetic counselling before and after testing.

In 2024 a common catalogue of genetic and genomic tests was also approved to reduce differences between regions, and a genomics network is being rolled out as part of the precision medicine plan.

What is not included

  • Tests without a medical reason: curiosity, ancestry, "nutrigenetics", sporting aptitude.
  • Expanded carrier screening in people without a family history, except in specific protocols.
  • Tests that have not shown clinical usefulness.

How to ask for one

  1. Talk to your GP or the specialist treating you.
  2. If they think it is indicated, they will refer you to a clinical genetics unit or order the test directly.
  3. Bring the reports of affected relatives and, if available, their genetic results.

Sources

  1. Order SSI/2065/2014 of 31 October amending the annexes of Royal Decree 1030/2006 (common portfolio of services of the Spanish NHS). BOE, 6 November 2014.
  2. Spanish Ministry of Health. NHS portfolio of services. sanidad.gob.es
