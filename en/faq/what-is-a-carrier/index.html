FAQ

What does it mean to be a carrier of a genetic condition?

Having one altered copy of a gene without having the condition. We are all carriers of something; it only matters if your partner carries the same thing.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 20 November 2026

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

Short answer

Being a carrier means having one altered copy of a gene and one normal copy. In recessive conditions the normal copy is enough to stay healthy, so the carrier does not have the condition and usually notices nothing. But they can pass the altered copy on to their children.

In this article

  1. Short answer
  2. A little more

A little more

We all have two copies of almost all our genes, one from each parent. It is estimated that every person carries between one and several serious recessive conditions without knowing it.

The problem arises when both partners carry the same condition. Then, in each pregnancy, there is a:

  • 25 % chance of an affected child.
  • 50 % chance of a healthy carrier child, like the parents.
  • 25 % chance of a child without the variant.

Some female carriers of X-linked conditions, such as haemophilia or Duchenne muscular dystrophy, can have mild symptoms because of how the X chromosome is inactivated.

Carrier screening tests are now available that check hundreds of conditions at once. They are most useful before trying for a pregnancy. See planning a pregnancy.
