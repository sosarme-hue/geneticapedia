FAQ

If my father or mother has a genetic condition, will I get it?

It depends on the type of inheritance, and it is almost never 100 % certain. How to work out the risk and when to be tested.

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

Short answer

It depends on how the condition is inherited, and it is almost never a certainty. In a dominant condition you have a 50 % chance of having inherited it. In a recessive one, you are most likely just a healthy carrier.

By type of inheritance

Type Examples If one parent has it...
Autosomal dominant Huntington's, Marfan, familial hypercholesterolaemia 50 % chance of inheriting it
Autosomal recessive Cystic fibrosis, SMA You will be a carrier; you would only have it if the other parent also passed on a copy
X-linked Haemophilia, Duchenne If your father has it: daughters will be carriers and sons healthy. If your mother is a carrier: 50 % for each son
Chromosomal Down syndrome Almost never inherited

Inheriting is not always becoming ill

Even if you inherit the variant, some conditions have incomplete penetrance: not all carriers become ill, as in haemochromatosis or hereditary cancer. And severity can vary a lot within the same family.

Should I be tested?

It is a personal decision, especially for conditions without treatment such as Huntington's disease, where many people prefer not to know. In others, knowing allows prevention, as in familial hypercholesterolaemia or Lynch syndrome. Genetic counselling helps you decide.
