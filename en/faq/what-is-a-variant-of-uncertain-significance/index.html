FAQ

What is a "variant of uncertain significance"?

A DNA change for which it is not yet known whether it causes disease. It is not a positive result and should not be used to make medical decisions.

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

Short answer

It is a change in a gene that the laboratory has found, but about which there is not yet enough information to know whether it causes a disease or is a harmless variation. It is not a diagnosis and should not be used to decide on treatment, preventive surgery or testing of relatives.

A little more

Laboratories classify variants into five groups, according to the international ACMG standards published in 2015:

  1. Pathogenic
  2. Likely pathogenic
  3. Uncertain significance (VUS)
  4. Likely benign
  5. Benign

Uncertain variants are very common, especially when many genes are tested at once and in people of ancestries under-represented in databases. Over time, as more people are studied, most are reclassified as benign: in a study of more than a million tests, over 90 % of reclassified variants became benign.

What to do

  • Do not panic or make decisions based on it.
  • Ask whether testing affected relatives could help clarify it (that can help).
  • Ask to be told if it is reclassified, or check back in a few years.

More in: How to read a genetic result.
