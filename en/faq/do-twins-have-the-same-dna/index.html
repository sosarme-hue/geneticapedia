FAQ

Do twins have the same DNA?

Identical twins have almost the same DNA, but not exactly. Non-identical twins are as genetically similar as any pair of siblings.

Short answer

  • Identical twins (monozygotic) come from a single fertilised egg that splits in two. Their DNA is almost identical, but not completely.
  • Non-identical twins (dizygotic) come from two different eggs and two different sperm. They are as genetically alike as any two siblings: they share on average half of their variants.

The small differences

After they separate, each twin's cells keep dividing and accumulate the odd copying error. An Icelandic study in 2021 of 387 pairs of identical twins found that they differ on average by about 5 variants that arose very early in development. Their epigenetics also drifts apart with age.

Why they are not the same

Although they share almost all their DNA, identical twins have different fingerprints, can differ in height or weight, and one can have a disease the other does not. The environment in the womb, chance during development and each twin's experiences all play a part.

Why science studies them

Comparing how alike identical twins are with non-identical twins makes it possible to estimate how much genes influence a trait, known as heritability. A large analysis of almost all twin studies over 50 years concluded that, on average, genes and environment weigh about the same in human traits.

See also: Genes and environment and Why are brothers and sisters different?.

Sources

  2. Polderman TJC, Benyamin B, de Leeuw CA, et al. Meta-analysis of the heritability of human traits based on fifty years of twin studies. Nat Genet. 2015;47(7):702-709. doi:10.1038/ng.3285
