Everyday genetics

Eye colour, blood group, lactose, coriander or height: what is genetic about the traits we all share.

01

Eye colour

Why two blue-eyed parents almost always have blue-eyed children, while two brown-eyed parents can have children with any eye colour. The real genetics of eye colour, more complex than the school version.

Read →02

Blood groups

The A, B, AB and O groups and the Rh factor are among the clearest examples of Mendelian inheritance. How they are inherited, why they matter for transfusions and pregnancy, and what to make of blood-type diets.

Read →03

Lactose intolerance

For mammals, the norm is to stop digesting milk after weaning. Adults who can drink it carry a recent mutation that spread with herding: one of the best examples of human evolution.

Read →04

Coriander, bitterness and other tastes

Why coriander tastes of soap to some people, why some find broccoli much more bitter than others, and why only some can smell asparagus in their urine. The genes of taste and smell.

Read →05

Height

Height is the classic polygenic trait: it depends on thousands of genetic variants and also on nutrition and health. Why Spaniards have grown more than ten centimetres in a century.
