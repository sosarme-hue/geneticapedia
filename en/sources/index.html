Sources

Sources and library

Where to read more, cross-check and verify: reliable genetics sources for patients, families and professionals, with what to know about each.

A reliable source does not replace your care team. If a result or a news story worries you, talk to your doctor.

Information for patients and families

MedlinePlus Genetics ↗
U.S. National Library of Medicine. Clear explanations of genetics and hundreds of conditions.
Orphanet ↗
European portal for rare diseases and orphan drugs, with a directory of patient organisations and expert centres.
Genetic Alliance UK ↗
UK charity alliance supporting people affected by genetic, rare and undiagnosed conditions.
NHGRI · Genome.gov ↗
U.S. National Human Genome Research Institute. Fundamentals, an illustrated glossary and educational resources.

Clinical information

GeneReviews ↗
Expert-reviewed monographs on the diagnosis, management and genetic counselling of each condition.
OMIM ↗
Catalogue of human genes and genetic disorders. Very technical, aimed at professionals.
National Cancer Institute · Cancer genetics ↗
Inherited cancer predisposition and tumour genetics.
NHS · Genomic medicine ↗
How genomic testing is organised in the NHS in England, including the National Genomic Test Directory.

Variants and interpretation

ClinVar ↗
Public database of variants and their classifications. Different laboratories may classify the same variant differently.
ClinGen ↗
Consortium that uses explicit criteria to assess which genes cause which diseases and how to classify variants.
gnomAD ↗
Variant frequencies in hundreds of thousands of people. Helps decide whether a variant is too common to cause a rare disease.

Medicines and treatments

European Medicines Agency (EMA) ↗
Assessment reports (EPARs) for medicines authorised in the European Union, including advanced therapies.
AEMPS · CIMA ↗
Summaries of product characteristics and leaflets for medicines authorised in Spain.
CPIC ↗
International guidelines that translate pharmacogenetic results into dosing recommendations.

Research

PubMed ↗
Search engine for biomedical articles. Finding an article is not finding evidence: the type and quality of study must be assessed.
Cochrane Library ↗
Systematic reviews that summarise all the available evidence on a question.
ClinicalTrials.gov ↗
International registry of clinical trials. A registered trial does not prove that a treatment works.
EU Clinical Trials (CTIS) ↗
Public search of clinical trials authorised in the European Union.

History

Watson and Crick (1953) · Nature ↗
"Molecular structure of nucleic acids", the one-page paper that proposed the double helix.
Franklin and Gosling (1953) · Nature ↗
"Molecular configuration in sodium thymonucleate", with the X-ray diffraction data.
Avery, MacLeod and McCarty (1944) · J Exp Med ↗
The paper that identified DNA as the hereditary material.
NHGRI · 1953 DNA Double Helix ↗
Historical context of the discovery of the double helix.

Geneticapedia

An encyclopedia of genetics for the general public: science, health and society. It does not provide diagnosis or individual genetic counselling.

Worried about a condition that runs in your family? Talk to your family doctor, who can refer you to a clinical genetics or genetic counselling service.
