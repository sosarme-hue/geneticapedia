Genetic conditions and syndromes

Clear fact sheets on genetic conditions and syndromes, inherited or not: cause, symptoms, inheritance, diagnosis, treatment and where to learn more.

Genetic

Caused by a change in the genes or chromosomes. It is not always inherited: Down syndrome, for example, almost always arises anew in that pregnancy.

Inherited

Passed from parents to children through a variant carried in the egg or sperm, such as cystic fibrosis or Huntington's disease.

Congenital

Present from birth, whether or not it is genetic. An infection during pregnancy can also cause a congenital problem.

Haemoglobin disorders are the most common serious inherited diseases in the world. Cause, symptoms, the link with malaria, diagnosis and treatments, including the first CRISPR therapy.

Spinal muscular atrophy

A severe childhood disease that went from having no treatment to having three in just a few years. Cause, types, inheritance and why early diagnosis changes everything.

Marfan syndrome

A connective tissue disorder affecting the aorta, eyes and skeleton. Cause, signs, diagnosis and why heart monitoring saves lives.

Familial hypercholesterolaemia

Very high cholesterol from childhood, passed from parents to children. It is common, underdiagnosed and effectively treatable. How to suspect, confirm and treat it.

Duchenne muscular dystrophy

The most common muscular dystrophy of childhood. Why it affects almost only boys, how it is diagnosed and what treatments exist today, from steroids to gene therapy.

Fragile X syndrome

The most common inherited cause of intellectual disability and the most common known genetic cause of autism. A repeat disorder that grows from generation to generation.

Read →12

Phenylketonuria

The first inborn error of metabolism that could be detected in every newborn. Thanks to the heel-prick test and a special diet, people who have it grow up with normal development.

Read →13

Hereditary haemochromatosis

The most common genetic disorder in people of European descent. The body absorbs too much iron and stores it for decades, but if it is caught in time it is treated with something as simple as taking blood.

Read →14

Neurofibromatosis type 1

One of the most common dominant genetic conditions, with café-au-lait spots and benign tumours on nerves. Since 2020 there has been a targeted drug, selumetinib, for its most difficult tumours.

Read →15

Turner syndrome

It affects only girls and women, who are missing all or part of one of their two X chromosomes. Short stature and ovarian insufficiency are its main features, and good follow-up changes the outlook.

Read →16

Klinefelter syndrome

The most common chromosomal condition in men, with an extra X chromosome. Many never find out, and it is often discovered because of infertility. Diagnosing it in time allows low testosterone to be treated.

Read →17

Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease

The most common inherited kidney disease. The kidneys fill with cysts over decades and it is a major cause of dialysis. A drug can now slow it down.

Read →18

Myotonic dystrophy

The most common muscular dystrophy in adults. Muscles do not relax properly after contracting, but it also affects the heart, eyes and brain. And it gets worse from one generation to the next.

Read →19

Prader-Willi and Angelman syndromes

Two very different conditions caused by the same stretch of chromosome 15. Which one appears depends on whether the change comes from the father or the mother: the best example of genomic imprinting.

Read →20

Tay-Sachs and other lysosomal diseases

More than 70 conditions in which the cell's "recycling centre" fails and substances build up. Tay-Sachs, Gaucher, Fabry and Pompe, and how they are treated today.

Read →21

Retinitis pigmentosa and Leber congenital amaurosis

Inherited retinal dystrophies are the leading cause of genetic blindness. More than 300 genes can be involved, and for one of them the United States approved, in 2017, the first gene therapy for an inherited disease.

Read →22

Ehlers-Danlos syndromes

A group of 13 connective tissue conditions with very loose joints, stretchy fragile skin and, in some types, a risk of arterial rupture. The most common type still has no known gene.

Read →23

Friedreich's ataxia

The most common inherited ataxia. A three-letter repeat in the frataxin gene damages nerves and the heart from adolescence. Since 2023 there has been a first approved treatment.

Read →24

Hereditary ALS

Between 5 and 10 % of cases of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis run in families. The C9orf72 and SOD1 genes are the most common, and since 2023 there has been a gene-targeted drug for SOD1, tofersen.

Read →25

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and long QT syndrome

Two inherited heart conditions that can cause sudden death in young, apparently healthy people. When a young person dies suddenly, testing their family saves lives.

Read →26

Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency

An inherited condition that damages the lungs and liver, and is greatly underdiagnosed. Spain has one of the highest frequencies in the world of the S variant, and smoking multiplies the damage.

Read →27

Rett syndrome

A brain development disorder that affects almost only girls. After a seemingly normal first year, they lose speech and the use of their hands. Mouse experiments showed it can be reversed, and in 2023 the United States approved the first drug.

Read →28

Achondroplasia

The most common cause of disproportionate short stature. Almost always due to a single change, always the same letter, in the FGFR3 gene. Since 2021 there has been a treatment that increases growth, vosoritide.
