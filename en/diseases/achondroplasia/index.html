Achondroplasia

The most common cause of disproportionate short stature. Almost always due to a single change, always the same letter, in the FGFR3 gene. Since 2021 there has been a treatment that increases growth, vosoritide.

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional or genetic counselling.

At a glance
Gene FGFR3, chromosome 4
Variant In 98 % of cases, the same one: G380R
Inheritance Autosomal dominant; about 80 % of cases are new
Frequency 1 in 20,000-30,000 births
Average adult height About 131 cm in men and 124 cm in women
Treatment Vosoritide since 2021 in growing children

What it is

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia, the group of conditions affecting bone growth. People with achondroplasia have short arms and legs relative to the trunk, a large head with a prominent forehead, short hands and normal intelligence. Their life expectancy is close to normal.

Cause: a brake pressed too hard

Long bones grow from growth plates of cartilage at their ends. The FGFR3 gene makes a receptor that acts as a brake on this growth. In achondroplasia, a variant keeps the brake permanently on, so bones grow less.

It is one of the most uniform variants in genetics: almost every case has the same change, a G replaced by an A at position 1138 of the gene, which changes amino acid 380. It is also one of the most frequent new mutations in the human genome.

How it is inherited

  • About 80 % of children with achondroplasia have average-height parents: the variant is new and almost always arises in the sperm. Its frequency rises with the father's age.
  • A person with achondroplasia has a 50 % chance of passing it to each child, as in all autosomal dominant inheritance.
  • If both parents have achondroplasia, there is a 25 % chance that the baby inherits two copies, a form that is lethal shortly after birth.

Complications

  • In babies: the opening at the base of the skull is narrow and can compress the spinal cord, and sleep apnoea may occur. They need MRI and sleep studies.
  • In children: frequent ear infections, bowed legs, motor delay (they walk later).
  • In adults: narrowing of the lumbar spinal canal, causing pain and leg weakness, and excess weight.

Vosoritide

Vosoritide mimics a natural molecule, C-type natriuretic peptide, which counteracts the FGFR3 signal. It is injected under the skin every day while the growth plates are open.

In the clinical trial, treated children grew about 1.6 cm more per year than those on placebo, and the effect is maintained years later. It was approved in the European Union and the United States in 2021 and later extended to young babies. Studies are looking at whether it also improves body proportions and medical complications, not just height. Other drugs are being investigated, such as infigratinib, taken by mouth.

A debate within the community

Not everyone with achondroplasia sees short stature as something to be treated. Some associations argue that what matters is accessibility and acceptance, and that treatment decisions should be made with full information and without pressure. Other families value the treatment. It is a decision for each family with their medical team.
