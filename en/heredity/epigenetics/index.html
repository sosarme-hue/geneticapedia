Epigenetics

The chemical marks that switch genes on and off without changing the DNA sequence. What is known and what is exaggerated.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 20 November 2026

In this article

  1. Above the DNA
  2. The main marks
  3. Well-established examples
  4. The environment leaves a mark
  5. What is exaggerated
  6. Sources

Above the DNA

Epi means "above". Epigenetics studies changes in the activity of genes that do not alter the sequence of letters in DNA. They are like notes in the margin of the recipe book: "don't use this one"; "use this one often".

The main marks

  • DNA methylation: a small chemical group (methyl) is added to certain cytosines, usually when followed by a guanine. It tends to switch the gene off.
  • Histone modifications: the proteins around which DNA is wound can carry chemical tags that compact it (gene silenced) or loosen it (gene accessible).
  • Non-coding RNAs: small RNA molecules that block or degrade particular messages.

Thanks to these marks, each cell type keeps its identity: a liver cell "remembers" it is a liver cell every time it divides.

Well-established examples

  • X-chromosome inactivation. In women, one of the two Xs is almost completely switched off in each cell, at random. The coat of tortoiseshell cats, with orange and black patches, is a visible consequence.
  • Genomic imprinting. Some genes are expressed only if they come from the father, or only if they come from the mother. When this fails, conditions such as Prader-Willi or Angelman syndromes appear, which affect the same region of chromosome 15 depending on which parent the change comes from.
  • Cancer. In many tumours, genes that put the brakes on cell growth are switched off by methylation. There are "epigenetic" drugs that try to reverse this in some blood cancers.

The environment leaves a mark

Diet, smoking, stress and age alter the epigenetic pattern. People conceived during the Dutch famine of 1944–1945 showed, six decades later, differences in methylation of the IGF2 gene compared with their siblings.

What is exaggerated

The internet is full of claims such as "you can reprogram your genes with your thoughts" or "your grandparents' traumas are in your DNA". Caution is advisable:

  • Epigenetic inheritance across generations has been shown in plants and some animals. In humans, the evidence is still limited and hard to separate from social and cultural factors.
  • Epigenetic marks change, but that does not mean they can be controlled at will or with supplements.
