Human evolution

The African origin of humanity

All living humans descend from populations that lived in Africa about 300,000 years ago. DNA, together with fossils, tells how we left and populated the planet.

At a glance
First Homo sapiens About 300,000 years ago, in Africa
Main exit from Africa About 50,000–70,000 years ago
Key genetic evidence The greatest human genetic diversity is in Africa
"Mitochondrial Eve" Common maternal-line ancestor, who lived in Africa about 150,000–200,000 years ago

What the fossils say

For decades it was debated whether modern humans arose in a single place or in several regions at once from earlier populations. The oldest Homo sapiens fossils are in Africa: at Jebel Irhoud (Morocco) remains have been dated to about 300,000 years ago, and in Ethiopia there are fossils from about 200,000 years ago.

What DNA says

In 1987 Rebecca Cann, Mark Stoneking and Allan Wilson compared the mitochondrial DNA of people from all over the world. This DNA is inherited only through the maternal line, without mixing, which makes it possible to reconstruct a family tree of humanity. Their conclusion was that all living people descend through the maternal line from a woman who lived in Africa about 200,000 years ago, whom the press called "Mitochondrial Eve".

It is important to understand this correctly: Eve was not the only woman of her time nor the first human. She is simply the most recent common ancestor through the maternal line; the lines of other women of her era were broken at some point because they had only sons or no descendants. In the same way there is a "Y-chromosomal Adam" through the paternal line.

Later studies using whole genomes have confirmed the general picture:

  • Africa has the greatest genetic diversity on the planet. Two people from different regions of Africa can be more genetically different from each other than a European and an Asian.
  • Outside Africa, diversity decreases with distance, as if each new migration carried only part of the variation of the population it came from. This is called the serial founder effect.

The great migration

There were early exits from Africa that left few traces, but almost all non-African people today descend from populations that left about 50,000–70,000 years ago. From there:

  • They reached Asia and, very soon, Australia, at least 50,000 years ago.
  • They settled Europe about 45,000 years ago.
  • They crossed the Bering Strait into the Americas about 15,000–20,000 years ago, or perhaps earlier.

On the way they met other human species, such as Neanderthals and Denisovans, and interbred with them. See Neanderthals and Denisovans.

A more complex origin

We now know that the origin of Homo sapiens was not a single point but a process in which different African populations, separated and reunited over hundreds of thousands of years, contributed to what we are. Some recent studies suggest that mixing with archaic human populations also happened within Africa.

See also: How DNA is used for dating, Why biological races do not exist and Svante Pääbo.
