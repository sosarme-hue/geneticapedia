Ángel Carracedo

The Galician physician who made Santiago de Compostela a world reference in forensic genetics and population genomics. He has helped identify disaster victims and missing people.

Who he is

Ángel Carracedo Álvarez was born in Santa Comba (A Coruña) in 1955. He is professor of legal medicine at the University of Santiago de Compostela, where he heads the Institute of Forensic Sciences, and has directed the Galician Public Foundation for Genomic Medicine, which provides genetic testing to Galicia's public health service. He is one of Spain's most cited researchers in his field.

Forensic genetics

In the late 1980s, shortly after Alec Jeffreys invented DNA fingerprinting, Carracedo began applying DNA analysis to court cases in Spain. His Santiago laboratory became an international reference:

  • He helped standardise the DNA markers used in Europe and validate mitochondrial DNA and Y-chromosome markers for highly degraded remains.
  • He was president of the International Society for Forensic Genetics.
  • He has taken part in identifying victims of major disasters and in searching for missing people in Latin America and victims of the Spanish Civil War.
  • He has developed methods to predict traits such as eye colour or geographical origin from DNA.

See DNA fingerprinting and Identifying victims.

Genomics and disease

His group also studies the genetic basis of common and rare diseases: autism, schizophrenia, cancer and infectious diseases. During the COVID-19 pandemic he coordinated Spanish and international studies on genes that influence the severity of infection. He has also promoted genomic medicine in the public system and research into the genetic structure of the Spanish population.

Why it matters

Carracedo is an example of how Spanish genetics has contributed worldwide in a field with huge social impact: solving crimes, clearing the innocent and restoring the identity of victims.
