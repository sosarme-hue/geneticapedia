## How to use these worksheets

Each worksheet fits on one or two sides of A4 and takes about **30-45 minutes**. Print them from the browser (File → Print): the printed version hides the menus. The **answers** are at the end, so teachers can print them separately. For practice on screen, the [quiz](../../quiz/) has a mode by level.

## Worksheet 1. DNA and its bases (ages 14-16)

Before you start: [What is DNA?](../../basics/what-is-dna/)

1. Write the complementary strand of this DNA fragment: **5′-ATGCCGTA-3′**.
2. If a DNA sample has 30 % adenine (A), what percentage of thymine (T), guanine (G) and cytosine (C) does it have? Hint: [Chargaff's rules](../../history/).
3. Fill in the gaps: DNA is found in the ______ of the cell, it is organised into ______ and each human cell has ______ of them.
4. Copy this template strand into messenger RNA: **3′-TACGGA-5′**. Which letter changes compared with DNA?
5. In your own words, explain the difference between a **gene**, a **chromosome** and a **genome**.

## Worksheet 2. Mendel problems (ages 15-16)

Before you start: [Mendel's laws](../../heredity/mendels-laws/).

In peas, yellow (**A**) is dominant over green (**a**).

1. We cross an **AA** plant with an **aa** plant. What will the offspring be like (genotype and phenotype)?
2. We cross two **Aa** plants. Draw the Punnett square. What proportion of green peas do you expect?
3. A yellow plant crossed with a green one gives half yellow and half green offspring. What was its genotype?
4. In a class, 20 pairs toss two coins 40 times (see [classroom activities](../classroom-activities/)). Why are one pair's results further from 3:1 than the whole class's?

## Worksheet 3. Family trees (ages 15-17)

Before you start: [Inheritance patterns](../../heredity/inheritance-patterns/).

!fig info-herencia-en.svg | Infographic of three inheritance patterns: autosomal dominant, recessive and X-linked | The three most common inheritance patterns.

1. A healthy couple have a child with [cystic fibrosis](../../diseases/cystic-fibrosis/), which is autosomal recessive. What are the parents' genotypes? What is the chance that their next child will also have it?
2. A father with [Huntington's disease](../../diseases/huntingtons-disease/) (autosomal dominant, heterozygous) and a healthy mother. What is each child's chance of inheriting the variant?
3. A woman who carries [haemophilia](../../diseases/haemophilia/) and a healthy man. What is the chance that a son has haemophilia? And a daughter?
4. Why does a father with haemophilia never pass the condition on to his sons?
5. Draw a three-generation tree for an invented family with a dominant condition, using the symbols in the infographic.

## Worksheet 4. Biotechnology: PCR and CRISPR (ages 16-18)

Before you start: [Types of genetic tests](../../diagnosis/types-of-genetic-tests/) and [CRISPR explained](../../treatments/crispr-explained/).

!fig info-pcr-en.svg | Infographic of PCR in three steps | The three steps of PCR and how copies double every cycle.

1. Put the three steps of a PCR cycle in order and explain them. Why do we need a polymerase that can withstand heat?
2. Starting from **one** DNA molecule, how many copies will there be, in theory, after **10 cycles**? And after **20**?
3. What are primers for? What would happen if they were designed for a region that is not in the sample?
4. In CRISPR-Cas9, what is the role of the **guide RNA** and what is the role of **Cas9**?
5. Explain the difference between repairing a cut **without a template** and **with a template**. Which would you use to switch a gene off, and which to correct a mutation?
6. Debate: why is editing embryos (germline editing) much more controversial than treating a patient's cells (somatic editing)? See [the ethics of gene editing](../../treatments/ethics-of-gene-editing/).

## Answers

**Worksheet 1.** 1) 3′-TACGGCAT-5′ (the strands are antiparallel: A pairs with T and G with C). 2) T 30 %, G 20 %, C 20 %. 3) Nucleus; chromosomes; 46. 4) 5′-AUGCCU-3′; uracil (U) replaces thymine (T). 5) A gene is a stretch of DNA with instructions for a function; a chromosome is a tightly packed DNA molecule carrying hundreds or thousands of genes; the genome is all the DNA of an organism.

**Worksheet 2.** 1) All **Aa**, yellow. 2) 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa; **1/4** green (25 %). 3) **Aa**. 4) Chance: with little data, deviations are larger; when many results are added up, the proportion gets closer to the expected one.

**Worksheet 3.** 1) Both are carriers (**Aa**); **25 %** in each pregnancy. 2) **50 %**. 3) Son: **50 %**; daughter: she will not have it, but has a 50 % chance of being a carrier. 4) Because sons get his **Y** chromosome, not his X.

**Worksheet 4.** 1) Separate the strands (≈95 °C), attach the primers (≈55-65 °C) and copy (72 °C); Taq polymerase comes from a hot-spring bacterium and is not destroyed at 95 °C. 2) 2¹⁰ = **1,024**; 2²⁰ ≈ **1 million**. 3) They mark the start and end of the piece to be copied; if the region is not there, nothing is copied (negative result). 4) The guide RNA finds the sequence; Cas9 cuts both strands. 5) Without a template, the cell joins the ends and often introduces errors that **switch the gene off**; with a template, it copies the sequence provided and can **correct** or insert. 6) Open answer: germline changes pass to future generations, who cannot consent, and the long-term risks are unknown.
