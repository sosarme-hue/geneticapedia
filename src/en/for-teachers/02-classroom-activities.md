## 1. Extract DNA from a strawberry or banana

**Level**: from age 14. **Time**: 30 minutes. **Materials**: strawberries (best frozen and thawed), washing-up liquid, salt, water, very cold surgical spirit or rubbing alcohol, zip-lock bags, a sieve or coffee filter, clear cups and cocktail sticks.

1. Mash 2-3 strawberries in the bag for two minutes.
2. Add a mix of 100 ml of water, a tablespoon of washing-up liquid and a pinch of salt. Mix gently.
3. Filter the liquid into a cup.
4. Slowly pour the same volume of **very cold alcohol** down the side of the cup.
5. A whitish cloud forms on the surface: it is **DNA** from millions of cells. It can be wound onto a stick.

**What happens**: the soap breaks cell and nuclear membranes, the salt helps DNA clump together and the alcohol makes it precipitate. Strawberries work very well because they have eight copies of each chromosome. To discuss: [What is DNA](../../basics/what-is-dna/) and the myth that [we share 60 % of our DNA with a banana](../../blog/do-we-share-60-of-our-dna-with-a-banana/).

**Safety**: alcohol is flammable; keep it away from heat.

## 2. Mendel with coins

**Level**: age 15-16. **Time**: 20 minutes. **Materials**: two coins per pair.

Each coin is a **heterozygous** parent (Aa): heads is A, tails is a. Each pair tosses both coins 40 times and records the result: AA, Aa or aa. Pooling the whole class's data, the ratio approaches **1:2:1**, and if A is dominant, **3:1**.

To discuss: why small groups stray from the expected result (chance), and why siblings [are not identical](../../faq/why-are-siblings-different/). Background: [Mendel's laws](../../heredity/mendels-laws/).

## 3. Build a pedigree

**Level**: age 15-18. **Time**: 45 minutes.

Give each group an invented family history, such as a family with haemophilia over three generations. They draw the pedigree using **standard symbols** (square for males, circle for females, filled if affected) and work out the inheritance pattern.

Case ideas: [haemophilia](../../diseases/haemophilia/) (X-linked), [Huntington's disease](../../diseases/huntingtons-disease/) (dominant) and [cystic fibrosis](../../diseases/cystic-fibrosis/) (recessive).

**Important**: use **invented** families, not students' own, so that no personal health information is exposed.

## 4. A paper or sweet double helix

**Level**: from age 14. **Time**: 40 minutes. **Materials**: card in four colours or sweets in four colours, cocktail sticks and liquorice.

Each colour is a base: A, T, G or C. The liquorice forms the two strands, and sticks with two sweets form the pairs: **A always with T, and G always with C**. Twisting the model shows the helix. One group can write a single-strand sequence and another build the complementary strand.

To discuss: the story of [Rosalind Franklin and Photo 51](../../blog/rosalind-franklin-and-photo-51/) and [Chargaff's rules](../../people/erwin-chargaff/).

## 5. Trait survey and myths

**Level**: age 12-18. **Time**: 30 minutes.

Run an anonymous class survey of traits: eye colour, whether they like coriander, whether cabbage tastes bitter. Then compare with the science in [Eye colour](../../everyday-traits/eye-colour/) and [Coriander, bitterness and other tastes](../../everyday-traits/coriander-and-taste/).

Use it to bust myths from old textbooks: **tongue rolling** and **attached earlobes** do not follow simple Mendelian inheritance, as taught for decades. Identical twins sometimes differ in them.
