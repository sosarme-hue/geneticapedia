## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **What gives colour** | The amount of melanin in the iris |
| **Main genes** | *OCA2* and *HERC2*, on chromosome 15 |
| **Other genes** | More than 60 contribute smaller effects |
| **Blue eyes** | Trace back to a variant that arose 6,000 to 10,000 years ago |
| **Inheritance** | Polygenic, not simple dominant-recessive |

## The school myth

For decades, pupils were taught that eye colour depended on a single gene, with brown **dominant** over **recessive** blue, just like [Mendel's](../../heredity/mendels-laws/) peas. In that model, two blue-eyed parents could never have a brown-eyed child. In reality it is **rare but possible**, because many genes are involved.

## How colour is made

The iris has no blue or green pigment. Everything depends on **melanin**, the same pigment found in skin and hair, made by cells called melanocytes:

- **Lots of melanin** in the front layer of the iris: **brown** eyes.
- **Very little melanin**: light scatters in the iris tissue, as it does in the sky, and the eyes look **blue**.
- **Intermediate amounts**, sometimes with a yellowish pigment: **green**, **hazel** or **grey** eyes.

Many European babies are born with light eyes that darken during the first year, as the melanocytes start making more pigment.

## The main genes

The most important gene is **OCA2**, which makes a protein needed to produce melanin. Next to it lies **HERC2**, which contains a "switch" controlling how strongly *OCA2* is turned on. In **2008**, a University of Copenhagen team found that almost all blue-eyed people share **the same variant** in that switch, which lowers *OCA2* activity in the iris. They concluded that it comes from **a single ancestor** who lived 6,000 to 10,000 years ago, probably around the Black Sea.

That variant explains much of the difference between blue and brown eyes in Europe, but not all of it. Genes such as *SLC24A4*, *TYR* and *IRF4* fine-tune the result, and a 2021 study of almost 195,000 people found **61 regions** of the genome linked to eye colour.

## Curiosities

- **Heterochromia**: eyes of different colours, or one eye with two colours. It is usually harmless, sometimes due to mosaicism, although it can be linked to some syndromes or injuries.
- **Albinism**: people with [oculocutaneous albinism](../../diseases/) caused by changes in *OCA2* or *TYR* have very little melanin and very light eyes, with impaired vision.
- **Forensic prediction**: from a DNA sample, blue or brown eyes can be predicted with more than 90 % accuracy. Green and intermediate colours are harder. See [DNA fingerprinting](../../forensic-genetics/dna-fingerprinting/).
