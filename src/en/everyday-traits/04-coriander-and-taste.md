## The genes of taste and smell

On the tongue we have receptors for five basic tastes: sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami. In the nose we have about **400 different olfactory receptor genes**, the largest gene family in the human genome. Many have very common variants that change what we perceive, so **each person lives in a slightly different world of smells and tastes**.

## Coriander that tastes of soap

Coriander, or cilantro, is one of the world's most used herbs, but for some people it tastes of **soap** or bugs. Between 3 % and 21 % of people dislike it, depending on ancestry: it is more common among Europeans and East Asians than among Latin Americans or South Asians, for whom it is part of everyday cooking.

In **2012**, a study by the company 23andMe of about 25,000 people found an association with a region of chromosome 11 containing several smell receptors, including **OR6A2**. That receptor detects the **aldehydes** that give coriander its smell, the same ones found in some soaps. But the variant's effect is small: other genes matter too, and above all **habit**. Many people who hated it come to enjoy it after eating it often.

## The bitterness of phenylthiocarbamide

In **1931**, chemist Arthur Fox was working with a substance called **phenylthiocarbamide** (PTC). A colleague complained of its bitter taste, but Fox could taste nothing. That is how it was found that the ability to taste it is **inherited**. For decades it was a classic genetics class experiment.

In **2003** the gene responsible was identified: **TAS2R38**, a bitter taste receptor. It has two main variants:

- **PAV**: the "taster" variant. People with two copies taste the bitterness intensely.
- **AVI**: the "non-taster" variant. People with two copies barely perceive it.

About **25-30 %** of Europeans are non-tasters. The same receptor detects compounds in **cabbage, broccoli, Brussels sprouts** and rocket, so it influences whether people like these vegetables, especially children.

## Other examples

- **Asparagus**: after eating it, urine smells of sulphur. Almost everyone produces the smell, but a 2016 study found that 60 % of men and 40 % of women of European ancestry **cannot smell it**, linked to variants in smell genes.
- **Androstenone**: a compound in sweat and boar meat. Depending on variants of the *OR7D4* gene, some people find it smells of urine, others of vanilla, and others cannot smell it at all.
- **Spicy heat**: the burn of chilli is not a taste but a sensation of pain and heat from the *TRPV1* receptor. Tolerance depends mainly on habit.
