## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Lactose** | The sugar in milk |
| **Lactase** | The enzyme that digests it, encoded by the *LCT* gene |
| **Lactase persistence** | Continuing to make it in adulthood, thanks to variants in the neighbouring *MCM6* gene |
| **Worldwide** | About two in three adults digest it poorly |
| **In Spain** | Around a third of adults have hypolactasia |

## Tolerating milk is the odd one out

All babies make **lactase**, the small-intestine enzyme that splits lactose into two simple sugars. In almost all mammals, and in most people, production **falls after weaning**. This is normal and not a disease.

When an adult with little lactase drinks a lot of milk, undigested lactose reaches the colon, where bacteria ferment it. That causes **wind, bloating, pain and diarrhoea**: the symptoms of **lactose intolerance**. Almost everyone affected tolerates small amounts, such as milk in coffee, and aged cheeses and yoghurt, which contain little lactose.

There is a very rare form, **congenital lactase deficiency**, in which the baby makes no lactase from birth. It is [autosomal recessive](../../heredity/inheritance-patterns/) and especially common in Finland.

## The herders' mutation

In some populations most adults keep making lactase: this is **lactase persistence**. In Europe it is mainly due to a single variant, called **-13910*T**, located not in the lactase gene itself but in a switch inside the neighbouring *MCM6* gene, which keeps *LCT* turned on.

It is one of the clearest examples of **natural selection** in humans. The variant spread very fast over the last few thousand years in cattle-keeping populations. Different variants, which arose independently, are found in herders in **East Africa** and the **Middle East**: a case of convergent evolution.

[Ancient DNA](../../human-evolution/atapuerca-and-ancient-dna/) studies brought a surprise: Europe's first farmers already consumed dairy products, but hardly carried the variant. It became common much later, in the Bronze Age and after. A 2022 study suggested that the advantage was greatest during **famines and epidemics**, when diarrhoea caused by milk could be deadly.

| Region | Adults who digest lactose well |
|---|---|
| Northern Europe | More than 90 % |
| Spain | About two thirds |
| West Africa, East Asia | Under 10 % in many populations |

## How it is diagnosed

- **Breath test**: the person takes lactose and hydrogen in the breath is measured.
- **Genetic test** for the -13910 variant: shows whether a European has lactase persistence, but not whether they have symptoms.

It should not be confused with **cow's milk protein allergy**, an immune reaction that is more common in babies and can be serious.
