## A false fight

"Nature or nurture?" is an old and badly framed question. Almost all human traits depend on **genes and environment at the same time**, and on how they interact.

A clear example is **phenylketonuria**. It is a purely genetic disease: it is caused by two altered copies of the *PAH* gene. Untreated, it causes severe intellectual disability. But if it is detected at birth with the heel-prick test and a low-phenylalanine diet is followed, development can be normal. The gene does not change; the environment changes everything.

## What "heritability" means

**Heritability** is a number between 0 and 1 that indicates what part of the **differences between people** in a particular population is explained by genetic differences.

Three common mistakes:

1. **It does not apply to individuals.** If the heritability of height is 0.8, it does not mean that 80 % of your height comes from your genes.
2. **It is not fixed.** It changes with the population and the environment. If everyone eats well, differences in height reflect genes more.
3. **Heritable does not mean unchangeable.** Short-sightedness is highly heritable and is corrected with glasses; moreover, its frequency has risen sharply in a few decades because of environmental changes, such as spending more time indoors.

## How it is studied

- **Twin studies**: compare identical twins (who share almost all their DNA) with non-identical twins (who share half on average).
- **Adoption studies**: compare children with their biological and adoptive families.
- **Genome-wide association studies (GWAS)**: look, in hundreds of thousands of people, for variants that appear more often in those with a trait or disease.

## Polygenic risk scores

Adding up the effect of thousands of variants gives a **polygenic risk score**. These can help research and, in some cases, refine the risk of coronary disease or some cancers. But they have limits:

- They predict less well in people of non-European ancestry, because most studies have been done in Europeans.
- They give population probabilities, not individual certainties.
- Classic factors (smoking, blood pressure, cholesterol, physical activity) still weigh heavily.

## The practical lesson

Having a high genetic risk for a common disease is one more reason to look after yourself, not a reason to give up. And having a low risk does not protect someone who smokes or does not move.
