## Short answer

**In the vast majority of cases, no.** Cancer arises from changes in DNA, but these are almost always changes a cell accumulates during life and that are not passed on to children. Only **5 to 10 %** of cancers are due to an inherited variant that greatly raises the risk.

## A little more

Several cases of cancer in one family are common, because cancer is common: around one in two or three people will have some cancer in their lifetime. Families also share habits such as smoking or diet.

It is worth suspecting **hereditary cancer** and asking for [genetic counselling](../../diagnosis/genetic-counselling/) when there are:

- Several close relatives on the same side of the family with the same type of cancer or related cancers (breast and ovary; bowel and womb).
- Cancers at **young ages**, for example before 50.
- One person with **two different cancers**, or in both paired organs (both breasts).
- Rare types, such as breast cancer in a man or ovarian cancer.

If a variant is found, as in [BRCA1 and BRCA2](../../diseases/hereditary-cancer/) or [Lynch syndrome](../../genetics-and-cancer/lynch-syndrome/), relatives can be tested and, if they carry it, follow check-ups that **save lives**.
