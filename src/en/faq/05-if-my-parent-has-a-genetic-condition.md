## Short answer

**It depends on how the condition is inherited**, and it is almost never a certainty. In a **dominant** condition you have a 50 % chance of having inherited it. In a **recessive** one, you are most likely just a healthy carrier.

## By type of inheritance

| Type | Examples | If one parent has it... |
|---|---|---|
| Autosomal dominant | [Huntington's](../../diseases/huntingtons-disease/), [Marfan](../../diseases/marfan-syndrome/), [familial hypercholesterolaemia](../../diseases/familial-hypercholesterolaemia/) | 50 % chance of inheriting it |
| Autosomal recessive | [Cystic fibrosis](../../diseases/cystic-fibrosis/), [SMA](../../diseases/spinal-muscular-atrophy/) | You will be a carrier; you would only have it if the other parent also passed on a copy |
| X-linked | [Haemophilia](../../diseases/haemophilia/), [Duchenne](../../diseases/duchenne-muscular-dystrophy/) | If your father has it: daughters will be carriers and sons healthy. If your mother is a carrier: 50 % for each son |
| Chromosomal | [Down syndrome](../../diseases/down-syndrome/) | Almost never inherited |

## Inheriting is not always becoming ill

Even if you inherit the variant, some conditions have **incomplete penetrance**: not all carriers become ill, as in [haemochromatosis](../../diseases/hereditary-haemochromatosis/) or [hereditary cancer](../../diseases/hereditary-cancer/). And severity can vary a lot within the same family.

## Should I be tested?

It is a personal decision, especially for conditions without treatment such as Huntington's disease, where many people prefer not to know. In others, knowing allows prevention, as in familial hypercholesterolaemia or Lynch syndrome. [Genetic counselling](../../diagnosis/genetic-counselling/) helps you decide.
