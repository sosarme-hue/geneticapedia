## Short answer

The risk **increases, but less than many people believe**. For any couple, the chance of having a child with a serious genetic condition or malformation is about **2–3 %**. For first cousins, roughly another **2 %** is added, bringing it to around **4–6 %**.

## Why it happens

We all carry some recessive variants. First cousins share a pair of grandparents, and therefore part of their genes (about 12.5 %). That makes it more likely that **both carry the same variant**, which is what a child needs to have a recessive condition. See [what is a carrier?](../what-is-a-carrier/).

The risk is higher when blood relationships repeat over several generations in a community.

## What is recommended

About **10 %** of marriages worldwide are estimated to be between relatives, in many regions by tradition. Genetics societies do not advise against them, but do recommend:

- **Genetic counselling** before pregnancy, reviewing conditions in the family.
- Considering **expanded carrier screening** for the couple.

See [planning a pregnancy](../../pregnancy-and-reproduction/planning-a-pregnancy/).
