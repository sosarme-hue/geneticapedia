title: In brief: a map of how genes raise the risk of heart attack
desc: A study published in Nature places much of the inherited risk of coronary artery disease in the cells of the artery wall.
---
**What happened.** On **25 November 2026** a team at Washington University in St. Louis (United States), led by Nathan Stitziel, published a map in *Nature* that connects inherited genetic variants to the genes and cells they act on in **coronary artery disease**, the cause of heart attacks.

**What it is.** Hundreds of [variants](../../basics/mutations-and-variants/) linked to this risk have been known for years, but most lie outside genes, in the DNA that controls when genes switch on. The team analysed 44 human coronary arteries cell by cell and studied how the DNA folds to see which gene each variant affects.

**Why it matters.** Much of the inherited risk acts in the **smooth muscle cells** of the artery wall, not only through cholesterol. That opens up new targets for treatment.

**Worth knowing.** This is basic research: it does not change diagnosis or treatment today. Heart attacks depend on many genes of small effect and, above all, on [diet, smoking, blood pressure and exercise](../../heredity/genes-and-environment/). [Familial hypercholesterolaemia](../../diseases/familial-hypercholesterolaemia/), caused by a single gene, is a different case.
