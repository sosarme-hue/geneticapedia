title: KJ, the first baby treated with bespoke gene editing
desc: In 2025 a Philadelphia team designed, in six months, a gene edit for a single baby with a deadly metabolic disease. A milestone that could change how rare diseases are treated.
---
## A very serious disease

**KJ Muldoon** was born in August 2024 in Philadelphia with severe **carbamoyl phosphate synthetase 1 (CPS1) deficiency**. This liver enzyme is needed to get rid of **ammonia**, a toxic by-product of protein digestion. Without it, ammonia builds up and damages the brain. Half of babies with the severe form die in the first months, and the only cure is a liver transplant.

## Six months to design a treatment

The team of **Kiran Musunuru** and **Rebecca Ahrens-Nicklas**, at the University of Pennsylvania and Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, sequenced KJ's genome and found his two variants. For one of them they designed a **base editor**, a version of [CRISPR](../../treatments/crispr-explained/) that changes a single DNA letter without cutting both strands.

The editor was packaged in **lipid nanoparticles**, like those in RNA vaccines, which travel to the liver after an infusion. They designed, made and tested it in animals in about **six months**, with special permission from the US medicines regulator. KJ received the first dose in February 2025, followed by two more.

## The results

The case was published in May 2025 in the *New England Journal of Medicine*. After treatment, KJ **tolerated more protein** in his diet, needed less medication to control ammonia, and got through infections without serious crises. It is too early to know the long-term effect, and follow-up continues.

## Why it matters

There are thousands of [rare diseases](../../rare-diseases/what-is-a-rare-disease/), many caused by variants found in only one family. No company would develop a drug for a single patient. KJ's case shows that a **platform** can be built: the same delivery system and type of editor, changing only the guide RNA for each variant. The challenge now is for regulators to approve that approach without repeating every trial for each patient.
