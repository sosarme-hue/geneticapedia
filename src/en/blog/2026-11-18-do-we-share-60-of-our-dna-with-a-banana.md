---
title: Do we share 60 % of our DNA with a banana?
desc: A figure that circulates everywhere and that, as usually told, is not true. What it really means.
---
It is one of the most repeated fun facts: "humans share 60 % of their DNA with bananas". It sounds surprising, which is why it gets shared. But as stated, it is **not correct**.

## Where the figure comes from

The number comes from comparing **genes**, not all of the DNA. It roughly says that about 60 % of human genes have a recognisable "cousin" gene in the banana, that is, a related gene descended from a very distant common ancestor. Many of these genes handle basic tasks shared by almost all living things: copying DNA, making proteins or producing energy.

A gene having a relative in the banana **does not mean** its sequence is the same. Often they are only partly similar.

## If we compare all the DNA

If we align whole genomes, the comparison with the banana makes almost no sense: most human DNA has no alignable equivalent in a plant. With closely related animals the figures are clearer:

- **Chimpanzee**: about **98.8 %** identity in the positions that can be aligned letter by letter. If added or lost segments are counted too, similarity drops to about **96 %**.
- **Between two people**: about **99.9 %** at the level of single letters, and somewhat less when structural variation is taken into account.

## The lesson

Behind this fact lies a beautiful and true idea: all living things are **related** and share a very old molecular toolkit. But percentages depend heavily on what is compared and how. When you see a figure like this, ask yourself: genes or DNA? Identical or similar?
