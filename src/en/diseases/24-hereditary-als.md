| **What ALS is** | A disease that destroys motor neurons and causes progressive paralysis |
| **Familial cases** | 5-10 %; genetic variants are also found in about 10 % of cases with no family history |
| **Main genes** | *C9orf72*, *SOD1*, *TARDBP*, *FUS* |
| **Inheritance** | Mostly autosomal dominant, with incomplete penetrance |
| **Targeted treatment** | Tofersen, for *SOD1* variants |

## What ALS is

**Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis**, also called motor neurone disease, destroys [motor neurons](../spinal-muscular-atrophy/), the nerve cells that tell muscles to move. It often begins with weakness in a hand or foot, or difficulty speaking or swallowing, and progresses to paralysis of all muscles, including those used for breathing. Median survival is 3 to 5 years from the first symptoms, although some people live much longer. About 900 new cases are diagnosed in Spain each year.

Most cases are **sporadic**: they appear with no family history. But in 5-10 % other relatives are affected[[4]], and this is called **familial ALS**.

## The genes

More than 40 related genes are known. The most important are:

| Gene | Year | Share of familial ALS in Europe | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***C9orf72*** | 2011 |[[2]] About 40 % | A six-letter repeat, GGGGCC, expanded hundreds or thousands of times. Also causes frontotemporal dementia |
| ***SOD1*** | 1993 |[[1]] About 15-20 % | The first gene discovered. More than 200 different variants |
| ***TARDBP*** | 2008 | About 4 % | Encodes TDP-43, the protein that builds up in almost all ALS |
| ***FUS*** | 2009 | About 4 % | Can cause aggressive juvenile forms |

Almost all are inherited in an **autosomal dominant** way, but with **incomplete penetrance**: not all carriers become ill, and age of onset varies a lot even within one family.

An important finding is that ALS and **frontotemporal dementia** form a spectrum: in a family with the *C9orf72* expansion, some people may have ALS, others dementia, and others both.

## The first gene-targeted treatment

**Tofersen** is an **antisense oligonucleotide** given by lumbar puncture that reduces production of the toxic SOD1 protein[[3]]. It was approved in the United States in **2023** and in the European Union in **2024** for people with ALS caused by *SOD1* variants. In the trial, effects on function took time to show, but a marker of nerve damage, **neurofilaments**, fell, and in the study extension those who started earlier did better.

An ongoing trial, **ATLAS**, gives tofersen to *SOD1* variant carriers **before symptoms appear**, when their neurofilaments start to rise, to try to prevent the disease. Other drugs for *C9orf72* and *FUS* are in trials.

The rest of ALS care is the same for everyone: riluzole, multidisciplinary clinics, ventilation, nutrition and augmentative communication.

## Should you be tested?

Today it is recommended to offer **genetic testing to every person with ALS**, with or without family history, because it can change treatment and help relatives. For healthy relatives, testing is a very personal decision, as in [Huntington's disease](../huntingtons-disease/): with incomplete penetrance, a positive result does not say whether or when someone will become ill. It should always be done with [genetic counselling](../../diagnosis/genetic-counselling/).

## What to ask at your appointment

Questions you can take with you. Not all of them will have an answer on day one, and your care team may add others.

- Has a genetic cause been found, such as *C9orf72* or *SOD1*?
- If it is *SOD1*, am I eligible for tofersen?
- Which multidisciplinary team will follow me, and how are breathing and nutrition managed?
- Are there clinical trials I could take part in?
- Should my relatives be tested, and with what support?
