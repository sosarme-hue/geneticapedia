## Who she is

Nancy Sabin Wexler was born in Washington in 1945. In 1968 her family learned that her mother, Leonore, had **Huntington's disease**, a **dominantly** inherited neurodegenerative disease: each child of an affected person has a 50 % chance of inheriting it. That same year her father, the psychoanalyst Milton Wexler, set up the **Hereditary Disease Foundation** to drive research. Nancy earned a PhD in clinical psychology and devoted her life to this disease.

## Venezuela

In 1979 she learned that the villages on the shores of **Lake Maracaibo** in Venezuela had the world's largest concentration of people with Huntington's disease, descendants of a woman who lived in the early 19th century. From 1981 she led yearly expeditions there that collected blood samples and reconstructed a **family tree** of more than 18,000 people over ten generations. The families knew her as "la catira", the blonde.

## The finding

Using those samples, in 1983 **James Gusella** and his team in Boston found a **genetic marker** on chromosome 4 that was inherited together with the disease. It was the first time a human disease gene had been located using DNA markers alone, with no knowledge of its function.

After ten years' work by an international consortium of six laboratories, the gene, ***HTT***, was identified in 1993. The mutation is an **expansion of CAG repeats**: people with 40 or more repeats will develop the disease.

## The decision to know

The discovery made a **predictive test** possible: finding out whether a healthy person will develop the disease. Wexler helped establish the protocols for **genetic counselling** before and after testing, now a model for other untreatable diseases. She herself chose not to be tested. In 2020 she revealed publicly that she had the disease.

## Why it matters today

Huntington's disease ushered in **predictive genetics** and the ethical debates that come with it: the right to know and the right not to know. Current trials of therapies that lower the huntingtin protein all start from the gene located thanks to the Maracaibo families.
