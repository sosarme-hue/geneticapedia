## Who he is

Lap-Chee Tsui (徐立之) was born in Shanghai in 1950 and grew up in Hong Kong. He studied biology at the Chinese University of Hong Kong and earned his PhD at the University of Pittsburgh. In 1981 he joined the **Hospital for Sick Children** in Toronto, Canada, one of the world's great children's hospitals, determined to find the **cystic fibrosis** gene.

## A huge challenge

Cystic fibrosis is the most common serious inherited disease in people of European descent, but in the 1980s nobody knew which protein was faulty. So the gene had to be found "blind", by following its inheritance in families: so-called **positional cloning**.

In 1985 Tsui's team, along with other groups, located the region on **chromosome 7**. But it spanned millions of bases, and the tools of the time were slow. Tsui joined forces with **Francis Collins**, then at the University of Michigan, who had developed a technique to "jump" along the chromosome, and with the biochemist **John Riordan**, also in Toronto.

## The finding

In August 1989 *Science* published **three papers** describing the gene, named ***CFTR***, and its most common variant: the loss of three bases that removes one amino acid, **phenylalanine 508** (now called F508del). It accounts for about 70 % of altered copies in people of European descent. The CFTR protein turned out to be a **chloride channel**, which explained the salty sweat and the thick secretions.

## Afterwards

Tsui continued to study the genome, especially chromosome 7. From 2002 to 2014 he was **Vice-Chancellor of the University of Hong Kong**. He has received many awards and is a Fellow of the Royal Society of London and a member of the Chinese Academy of Sciences.

## Why it matters today

Knowing the *CFTR* gene enabled genetic diagnosis, carrier testing and newborn screening for cystic fibrosis. Above all, understanding how the protein fails made it possible to design **CFTR modulators**, such as the combination of elexacaftor, tezacaftor and ivacaftor, which have transformed the lives of most patients.
