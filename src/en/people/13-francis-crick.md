## Who he was

Francis Harry Compton Crick was born in Northampton, England, in 1916. He studied physics at University College London and during the Second World War designed **magnetic mines** for the Admiralty. After the war, like Wilkins, he turned to biology. In 1949 he joined the Medical Research Council unit at the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge, where he was working on a thesis on protein structure. He was 35 and still had no doctorate when he met James Watson.

## The double helix

Crick brought his background in **diffraction theory** to the partnership. He understood that Rosalind Franklin's data indicated two strands running in **opposite directions** (antiparallel) and saw the meaning of Chargaff's rules. On 28 February 1953, legend has it, he walked into The Eagle pub in Cambridge announcing that they had "found the secret of life".

## How DNA is read

Crick's most important work came afterwards:

- **Adaptor hypothesis (1955).** He proposed that a small molecule would act as a "translator" between RNA and amino acids. It was soon discovered: **transfer RNA**.
- **Central dogma (1958).** He stated that information flows from DNA to RNA to proteins, but not from proteins back to nucleic acids. It is still the framework of molecular biology.
- **The triplet code (1961).** With Sydney Brenner, Leslie Barnett and Richard Watts-Tobin he showed, using mutations in a virus, that the genetic code is read in groups of **three bases**, without overlaps and from a fixed starting point.

## Recognition and later life

In 1962 he shared the **Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** with Watson and Wilkins. In 1976 he moved to the Salk Institute in California and turned to **neuroscience**, searching for the biological basis of consciousness. He died in 2004. The Francis Crick Institute in London, opened in 2016, is one of Europe's largest biomedical research centres.

## Why it matters today

Whenever a genetic report says that a variant changes a **codon** and alters an amino acid, it is applying the logic Crick helped establish.
