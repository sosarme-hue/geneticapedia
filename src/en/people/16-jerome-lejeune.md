## Who he was

Jérôme Lejeune was born in Montrouge, near Paris, in 1926. He studied medicine in Paris and in 1952 began working with paediatrician **Raymond Turpin** at the Trousseau Hospital, who had long suspected that Down syndrome had a chromosomal cause.

## Trisomy 21

In 1956 it was established that humans have **46 chromosomes**. In Turpin's laboratory, the young doctor **[Marthe Gautier](../marthe-gautier/)**, who had learnt cell culture in the United States, set up the technique for obtaining chromosomes from fibroblasts of children with Down syndrome. Lejeune photographed the preparations and counted **47 chromosomes**: there was one extra small chromosome.

The paper was published in January **1959** in the journal of the French Academy of Sciences, signed by **Lejeune, Gautier and Turpin**. It was the first time a human disease had been linked to a chromosomal abnormality. For decades the credit went almost entirely to Lejeune; in 2009 Gautier published her account, claiming a central role in the discovery, and in 2014 an INSERM ethics committee acknowledged her contribution.

## Other discoveries

In **1963** Lejeune described **cri du chat syndrome**, caused by loss of part of chromosome 5, in which babies have a characteristic high-pitched cry. His team also described other chromosomal abnormalities. He became the first professor of fundamental genetics at the Paris Faculty of Medicine.

## A controversial figure

Lejeune was a devout Catholic and firmly opposed **abortion** and the use of prenatal diagnosis to end pregnancies of fetuses with Down syndrome. These positions set him against many colleagues. Pope John Paul II named him the first president of the Pontifical Academy for Life shortly before his death, and the Catholic Church has opened his cause for beatification.

## Why it matters today

The discovery of trisomy 21 founded **clinical cytogenetics**: the study of chromosomes to diagnose disease. Today it underpins the karyotype, [prenatal screening](../../diagnosis/newborn-and-prenatal-screening/) tests and the diagnosis of many syndromes.
