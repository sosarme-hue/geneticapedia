## Who he was

Erwin Chargaff was born in 1905 in Czernowitz, in Bukovina, then part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire and now Chernivtsi, Ukraine. He studied chemistry in Vienna, worked at Yale, Berlin and Paris and, being Jewish, emigrated to the United States in 1935 as Nazism rose. He spent his whole career at Columbia University in New York. Besides being a biochemist he was a cultured, ironic writer, highly critical of the direction of modern science.

## The finding

Avery's 1944 paper convinced him that DNA was the molecule of heredity, and he refocused his laboratory to study it. At the time, according to the "tetranucleotide hypothesis", DNA was thought to be a monotonous repetition of its four bases in equal amounts.

Chargaff used a new technique, **paper chromatography**, to separate and measure the bases (adenine, thymine, guanine and cytosine) in DNA from different species: yeast, bacteria, ox, human, salmon. He found two regularities, published in 1950 and known as **Chargaff's rules**:

1. DNA composition **varies from one species to another**, but is the same in different tissues of the same species. DNA is not monotonous: it can carry information.
2. In every species, the amount of **adenine equals that of thymine** (A = T) and **guanine equals cytosine** (G = C).

## Meeting Watson and Crick

In May 1952 Chargaff visited Cambridge and met Watson and Crick. He thought them poorly prepared in chemistry, but explained his rules. Crick understood that A = T and G = C would make sense if the bases **paired up two by two**. That pairing is the heart of the double helix and the key to how DNA is copied.

Chargaff did not share the 1962 Nobel Prize and never hid his bitterness. In his autobiography, *Heraclitean Fire* (1978), he criticised molecular biology for becoming, in his view, a science of competition rather than reflection.

## Why it matters today

Chargaff's rules are in every textbook. The **GC content** of a genome is used today to identify bacterial species and to design the probes used in genetic tests.
