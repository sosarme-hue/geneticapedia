## Why contact an association

When a diagnosis of a rare disease arrives, many families feel alone. **Patient organisations** offer something a medical appointment cannot always provide:

- **Contact with other families** who have been through the same thing.
- **Practical information**: procedures, benefits, schools, home adaptations.
- **Psychological support**, physiotherapy, speech therapy or respite activities.
- **Advocacy** with public authorities.
- **Driving research**: many fund projects and run patient registries, which are essential for clinical trials.

Some have been decisive. In Spain, for example, pressure from families affected by [spinal muscular atrophy](../../diseases/spinal-muscular-atrophy/) has been key to it starting to be included in newborn screening.

## General federations

- **FEDER**, the Spanish Federation of Rare Diseases: brings together more than 400 associations. It runs a **free information and guidance service** and a directory of associations by disease. [enfermedades-raras.org](https://www.enfermedades-raras.org/)
- **EURORDIS**, the European alliance of rare disease patient organisations. [eurordis.org](https://www.eurordis.org/)
- **COCEMFE**, the Spanish Confederation of People with Physical and Organic Disabilities.
- **Plena inclusión**, for people with intellectual disabilities and their families in Spain.

## How to choose a good source

Associations are an excellent source, but be wary of any group, website or person that:

- Promises **miracle cures** or unapproved treatments for money.
- Advises against established medical treatments.
- Does not say who funds it.
