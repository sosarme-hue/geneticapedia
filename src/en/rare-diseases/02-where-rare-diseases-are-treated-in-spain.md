## The way in

Care begins, like any other, with the **family doctor** or **paediatrician**, who refers to the relevant specialist. When a genetic disease is suspected, the specialist can order tests or refer to a **clinical genetics unit**. Genetic tests with a medical indication are covered by public healthcare (see [Does public healthcare cover genetic tests?](../../faq/does-public-healthcare-cover-genetic-tests/)).

## Reference centres (CSUR)

For uncommon diseases or those requiring highly specialised techniques, the Ministry of Health designates **Reference Centres, Services and Units** (CSUR). These are units in specific hospitals with proven experience in a disease or group of diseases: for example inherited metabolic diseases, rare neuromuscular diseases, inherited heart conditions or skeletal dysplasias.

Any patient from any region can be **referred to a CSUR** by their doctor, even in another region, through the **Health Cohesion Fund**. The up-to-date list is on the Ministry of Health website.

## European Reference Networks (ERN)

Since **2017** the European Union has set up **24 European Reference Networks**, each dedicated to a group of rare diseases. They link hundreds of hospitals across Europe, many of them Spanish. Their main advantage is that **experts consult each other on difficult cases** through a virtual platform, so that "knowledge travels, not the patient".

## Spanish National Rare Diseases Registry (ReER)

Created by **Royal Decree 1091/2015**, it collects information on people with rare diseases across the country to find out how many there are, where they live and how their conditions progress, and to plan resources. There are also regional registries and patient registries run by associations.

## Research: CIBERER and IIER

- **CIBERER** (Biomedical Research Network Centre for Rare Diseases), created in 2006 by the Carlos III Health Institute, brings together dozens of research groups from all over Spain. It has described new disease genes and coordinates diagnostic programmes.
- The **Rare Diseases Research Institute** (IIER), also part of the Carlos III Health Institute, manages the patient registry and a **programme for undiagnosed cases**, which uses genomic techniques to study people who have gone years without an answer.
- The **IMPaCT** precision medicine programme includes genome sequencing for people with undiagnosed rare diseases.

## Social care: CREER

The **National Reference Centre for Care of People with Rare Diseases and their Families** (CREER), in **Burgos**, is run by IMSERSO. It offers respite stays for families, rehabilitation, training for professionals and support for associations.

## National strategy

Since **2009** Spain has had a **Rare Diseases Strategy of the National Health System**, updated in 2014, setting goals for prevention, early diagnosis, care, research and social support. Several regions also have their own plans.
