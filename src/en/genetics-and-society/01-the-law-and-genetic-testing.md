## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Main law** | Law 14/2007 of 3 July on Biomedical Research (Spain) |
| **International framework** | Oviedo Convention (1997), in force in Spain since 2000 |
| **Key principles** | Written consent, genetic counselling, confidentiality, right not to know, non-discrimination |
| **Also regulates** | Biobanks, research with embryos and cells, biological samples |

## Why a law is needed

A genetic test is not like a cholesterol test. It reveals information that **never changes during life**, that can **predict** future diseases and that also affects **relatives**, who have not given their consent. That is why almost every country has set specific rules.

## The Oviedo Convention

The **Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Dignity of the Human Being with regard to the Application of Biology and Medicine**, signed in Oviedo in **1997** under the Council of Europe, was the first binding international treaty on bioethics. Spain ratified it and it came into force in 2000. Among other things it establishes that:

- Any form of **discrimination** on grounds of genetic heritage is prohibited (Article 11).
- **Predictive genetic tests** may be carried out only for health purposes or health-related research, and with genetic counselling (Article 12).
- Interventions on the human genome are allowed only for preventive, diagnostic or therapeutic purposes and **without modifying the genome of descendants** (Article 13). See [ethics of gene editing](../../treatments/ethics-of-gene-editing/).
- Assisted reproduction may not be used to **choose a child's sex** except to avoid a serious hereditary disease (Article 14).

## Law 14/2007

Spain's **Biomedical Research Law** develops these principles. Its main points on genetic testing are:

- **Express, written consent**, after receiving information about the purpose of the test, what it may reveal and its implications.
- Mandatory **genetic counselling** when a test is done for health purposes, especially predictive tests. See [genetic counselling](../../diagnosis/genetic-counselling/).
- The **right not to be informed** of results (the "right not to know"), except when that information is needed to prevent serious harm to relatives' health. In that case, the doctor may inform the relatives concerned, which is a delicate balance.
- **Quality**: tests must be carried out in accredited centres that meet quality requirements.
- **Confidentiality** and a ban on using genetic data for purposes other than those for which they were obtained.
- **Non-discrimination** on grounds of genetic characteristics.

The law also regulates **biobanks**, collections of samples for research, and research with **embryos** and stem cells.

## Unexpected findings

With exome and genome sequencing it is common to find **secondary findings**: variants unrelated to the reason for the test that may nonetheless be important, such as a *BRCA1* variant in a child tested for epilepsy. The law and professional societies recommend that, before testing, people decide **whether they want to be told** about such findings.

## Direct-to-consumer tests

Spanish law requires genetic counselling and medical supervision for tests done for health purposes, which clashes with [direct-to-consumer tests](../../diagnosis/direct-to-consumer-genetic-tests/) sold online. In practice many are offered from other countries and the law is hard to enforce. The **EU In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation** (2017/746), applicable since 2022, has tightened the requirements for these tests.
