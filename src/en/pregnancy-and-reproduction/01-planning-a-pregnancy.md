## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **When to start** | Ideally several months before trying to conceive |
| **First step** | A genetic counselling appointment, requested through your GP or specialist |
| **Possible tests** | Testing for the family variant, carrier screening, testing the partner |
| **Options** | Natural conception with or without prenatal diagnosis, IVF with preimplantation testing, egg or sperm donation, adoption |

## Why planning helps

Many couples find out they have a genetic risk when they are already pregnant, with little time to think and fewer options. If there is a family history, **seeking advice before pregnancy** makes it possible to know the real risk, do the tests calmly and choose without pressure.

You should ask for advice if:

- One partner has a genetic condition, or a previous child has had one.
- There is a known hereditary condition in the family: [cystic fibrosis](../../diseases/cystic-fibrosis/), [spinal muscular atrophy](../../diseases/spinal-muscular-atrophy/), [haemophilia](../../diseases/haemophilia/), [Duchenne muscular dystrophy](../../diseases/duchenne-muscular-dystrophy/), [fragile X syndrome](../../diseases/fragile-x-syndrome/), [Huntington's disease](../../diseases/huntingtons-disease/)...
- Relatives have intellectual disability, malformations or unexplained deaths in childhood.
- There have been **several miscarriages**, which are sometimes due to a balanced chromosome translocation in one parent.
- The couple are **blood relatives** (cousins, for example), which raises the chance of sharing recessive variants.
- You belong to a population in which a particular condition is especially common, such as thalassaemia in the Mediterranean or Tay-Sachs disease in Ashkenazi Jews.

## The genetic counselling appointment

[Genetic counselling](../../diagnosis/genetic-counselling/) is provided by hospital clinical genetics services. There:

1. The **family tree** of both families is drawn.
2. Medical reports are reviewed and, where they exist, the **genetic test results** of affected relatives. This step is essential: to test the couple, it is almost always necessary to know the family's **specific variant** first.
3. The **risk** for each pregnancy is calculated according to the inheritance pattern (see [inheritance patterns](../../heredity/inheritance-patterns/)).
4. The options are explained, without steering the decision.

## Risks by type of inheritance

| Situation | Risk for each child |
|---|---|
| One parent with a dominant condition | 50 % |
| Both parents carriers of the same recessive condition | 25 % affected, 50 % healthy carriers |
| Mother a carrier of an X-linked condition | 50 % of sons affected, 50 % of daughters carriers |
| One parent with a balanced translocation | Variable; higher risk of miscarriage and imbalances |
| No family history | About 2–3 % risk of some congenital anomaly, as in the whole population |

Each pregnancy is independent: already having an affected child **does not change** the chance for the next one.

## Carrier screening

Even without a family history, expanded [carrier screening](../../diagnosis/carrier-and-preimplantation-testing/) can test for hundreds of recessive conditions at once. Almost everyone is a carrier of something. What matters is whether **both partners** carry the same condition. In Spain it is not generally offered by the public health system and is usually available at fertility clinics. Professional societies recommend that, if done, it should come with genetic counselling.

## Reproductive options

When there is a known risk, couples can choose:

1. **Natural conception without tests**, accepting the risk.
2. **Natural conception with [prenatal diagnosis](../prenatal-diagnosis-step-by-step/)**: chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis to find out whether the fetus has inherited the condition, and decide with that information.
3. **[IVF with preimplantation testing](../ivf-with-preimplantation-testing/)**: embryos are tested before being transferred to the womb.
4. **[Egg or sperm donation](../egg-and-sperm-donation/)** from a person who does not carry the variant.
5. **Adoption** or fostering.

No option is better than another: it depends on each couple's values, beliefs, age, the severity of the condition and their circumstances.

## General care before pregnancy

Apart from genetics, a preconception visit is used to:

- Take **folic acid** for at least a month before conception and during the first trimester. It reduces the risk of neural tube defects such as spina bifida by about 70 %, as a large British trial showed in 1991.
- Check vaccinations (rubella, chickenpox) and medicines.
- Manage conditions such as diabetes, epilepsy or [phenylketonuria](../../diseases/phenylketonuria/), which should be well controlled before conception.
- Stop smoking and drinking alcohol.
